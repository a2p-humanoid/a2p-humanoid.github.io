Abstract

Humanoid vision-language-action (VLA) models rely on visual feedback for manipulation, yet persistent camera contamination can continuously obscure task-relevant information. Such occlusions present two challenges: recovering initially missing scene information and updating the recovered observations as robot hands and objects move during manipulation. We propose an “active-to-predictive visual recovery framework” that addresses these challenges without corruption-specific VLA retraining. Our approach first exploits constrained humanoid waist movements to estimate camera-fixed contamination and acquire complementary viewpoints. The observations are integrated into a 3D scene memory and reprojected into the original camera viewpoint to initialize visual recovery. During manipulation, a corruption-conditioned, action-conditioned world model recurrently updates occluded regions using previously recovered observations, current partial camera evidence, and executed robot motions. The predicted content is combined with directly observed image regions and provided to a frozen VLA for closed-loop manipulation. Experiments on simulated and real-world Unitree G1 manipulation tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework under persistent camera contamination. Across four simulation tasks, our method improves average manipulation success from 21.5% to 43.0% compared with directly using corrupted observations, with additional improvements demonstrated on the real humanoid.

The problem

A smudge that never moves out of the way

The occluder is attached to the lens, so it moves with the camera. Unlike an object in front of the scene, simply re-looking does not help the policy: whatever the VLA sees at its training viewpoint stays hidden for the entire episode, and the hands and objects keep changing behind it.

  • Initially missing — the target or the goal may be hidden before the robot even starts.
  • Continuously outdated — as the arm moves, anything recovered once is stale a few steps later.
  • Viewpoint-sensitive policy — manipulating from a new viewpoint breaks the VLA's training distribution.

Method

Four stages: two that actively gather what the occluder hides before manipulation, and one that keeps predicting it during manipulation.

1

Look around — estimate the screen-fixed contamination

The robot sweeps its waist yaw by about ±0.3 rad and returns home (6.6 s). The scene slides across the image, but the contamination stays put. SAM 2 proposes segments in each frame; segments that persist at the same image location across frames with different yaw vote for the mask M. No contamination-specific segmentation model is trained.

2

Reveal — actively acquire views

An area-based next-best-view planner scores 204 candidate waist poses (yaw × roll × pitch) by how much of the initially hidden area each would newly expose, discounted by motion time. It uses no object detector and no training, and acquires at most four views.

Same recovery pipeline, different planners: NBV-Area reaches the highest success (48%) with 2.36 views on average, versus 36–42% for fixed, random, target-driven, and learned (PPO) selection.

3

Remember — build a canonical scene memory

Every look-around frame is lifted to 3D with Depth Anything 3, placed with forward-kinematics camera poses, and stripped of contaminated and robot pixels. Reprojecting this memory into the original home camera recovers what was behind the occluder — the robot then returns home, so the VLA keeps its training viewpoint. Explore the memory in 3D ↓

4

Act — continuously update the observation

A geometric snapshot goes stale as soon as the hand moves. We adapt the DreamDojo world model to be corruption-conditioned: at every policy query it combines the previous recovered image, the current partial camera image, the contamination mask, a rendering of the arm, and the executed joint trajectory to predict the occluded region. The prediction is fused with the directly visible pixels and handed to the frozen VLA.
